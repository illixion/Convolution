# AI 3D edge artifacts: Nope, Alpha, and House

This records the 2026-10-10 through 2026-10-13 headset investigation so future renderer work starts from the measured failures. The reference shot was the horse and corral in *Nope* around 4:00. *Alpha* exhibited the same original edge-stretching class, but the controlled A/B captures in the final round were from *Nope* and *House M.D.*; the final settings were not separately verified on *Alpha*.

The agent transcripts show that this investigation started earlier, on October 10, and included several rounds of fixes on October 11 and 12. The chronology below is reconstructed from those transcripts and checked against the resulting code. It includes failed and superseded approaches because their failure modes are the main reason to keep this report.

Transcript sources: `agent-sessions show 4d8b14ad --no-tools` (Claude, October 10–12) and `agent-sessions show 01a13091 --no-tools` (Codex, October 12–13). Git commits and the local capture manifests are stronger evidence for what actually landed and what was compared; agent discussion by itself is not proof of a fix.

## Earlier headset observations and experiments

**October 10: resolution, performance and strength.** The *Nope* film was already on the plugin's converted HDR10 1080p tier (`hvc1 1920×870`, PQ/BT.2020), so accidentally staying at 4K did not explain the static outline error. Eye-frame delivery was only 65–73% of the 24 fps source in several reports, which explains judder but not a visibly wrong *paused* frame. In a live strength test at the still horse shot, 0.75× still felt too separated, 0.5× was better but still far, and 0.35× fused comfortably. The same 0.35× made *House* look flat. A single lower global default was therefore rejected. The old strength menu had started at what was 1× in this test and offered only stronger settings; pinning it had limited further worsening but had not fixed the underlying scene difference.

A proposed shot-aware strength rule was measured before application. Across roughly 35 shots in *Nope*, *House*, and *Alpha*, the mean depth ranged 0.24–0.61. A mean-based policy predicted roughly 0.4–0.77×, including 0.43–0.57× for *Nope* shots where 0.35× was comfortable. It failed to distinguish open landscapes from interiors and **was not shipped**. Monocular depth is relative: both a room and a close rider against far hills can span nearly the full 0–1 range. The user pinpointed the defect at *Nope* 4:00 (face), *Alpha* 38:48 (transition from the stone to distant background on the right), and *House* S1E22 18:08 (near face's left edge). A sharp-step census found only about 0.1–0.5% of pixels on sharp near/far steps in those shots: the model had made a 10–30 px intermediate-depth ramp instead. That ramp rubber-sheeted the picture. The stone and room regions, where depth changes gradually, looked far better. Strength reduction merely hid the problem by flattening good scenes.

**October 10–11: first edge snap and background fill.** `depthEdgeSnap` used picture colour to assign the model's soft outline to subject or background. On a synthetic edge, an 18.5 px ramp became a sharp step. A wider search later reduced a low-contrast 35 px ramp to about 2 px in a Mac test. `meshEdgeFill` repeated nearby background into strips the warped subject uncovered. The first headset pass felt more comfortable, but part of the ear disappeared in one eye: fill classified a folded foreground mesh region as an exposed background hole and painted over it. Excluding folded cells restored the ear. Then the user saw sawtooth contours. The snap had made per-texel decisions on a smooth diagonal, and even a smoother colour-mix version did not make it universally reliable.

The right-eye spikes were isolated by turning fill off while leaving snap on. Spikes vanished, but the user said the two eyes again felt different, especially reflections. The fill's stretch estimate had used screen derivatives evaluated in 2×2 pixel blocks; the decision changed from row to row. Moving the stretch estimate to mesh corners and blending it across cells addressed that spike mechanism. This is why an early session briefly favored fill, while the later *Nope* jacket A/B with a different model/settings favored leaving it off. Neither headset observation should be erased; the heuristic was content-dependent.

**October 11: both-eye capture and letterbox depth statistics.** The `stereoEyes` debug endpoint was added to capture the exact warped left/right images, their source and depth map, rather than relying on a system screenshot. Captures showed that Vision's `.scaleFit` padding in the original 518×392 model came back as *near* depth. On a roughly 2.2:1 film, padding occupied about 40% of the model output and polluted shot statistics, anchoring and cut decisions. Cropping the padding from depth analysis put the rider at the near end and the hills at the far end; before, the rider sat around half depth. That correction made the left-eye shirt/beard/collar stretch **worse**, because the true near/far gap now used the full budget, about 15 px per eye in a 1920 px frame. Keep the letterbox fix, but do not interpret the earlier, artificially compressed depth as a good outcome.

**October 11: per-shot empty-gap compression.** `DepthGapCompressor` squeezes sparsely occupied depth ranges while keeping the depth variation inside an occupied subject. A synthetic subject/background range fell to roughly half its former span. On the headset, the user called *Nope* “a bit better but still fake”; right-eye stair-stepping remained. It stays on, but cannot reconstruct missing background or correct pixels the model put halfway between shirt and hills.

**October 11–12: repeated snap revisions.** The first snap used single extreme colour samples; hair, shirt folds and textured hills changed those samples from row to row and produced ragged outlines. Averaging near/far colours helped a synthetic test. A horizontal-only pass avoided pulling the corral steps up to a rail, but left parts of the steps near; a vertical pass pushed them back correctly in the test, then created patches in sloping ground. A median removed tiny specks. The user reported shimmering on another film's lower-left edge: the snap/fill result was recomputed *after* the depth temporal stabilizer, so fine fabric and grain made it flicker. A motion-sensitive temporal hold was added to the cleanup result.

Dark cap against dark trees exposed the deeper failure. The row pass sometimes picked an edge *inside the head* (cap brim, neck or ear) because subject and background colours matched. Background depth appeared inside the head, and fill painted trees into it, leaving a head-shaped outline with missing interior. Similar-colour errors recurred on a pale shirt against pale hills and tan glove fingers against tan hills. Connectivity tests, centre/outward bias, a stronger fill threshold (1.4× to 1.2× stretch), and special glove/rail tests each improved a frame or a synthetic fixture but exposed a different one. The user-supplied headset screenshot and both-eye captures overruled several “clean” synthetic results. Do not resume by adding another colour-only classification rule without a new source of information or much broader real-film evidence.

**October 12: distinct incidents.** The user switched from *Nope* to *The Matrix Resurrections* after cancelling a download and saw an old *Nope* 2D frame with disabled controls. The plugin could serve that MP4's object sound but not its picture index (404); the app now routes such a film to the ordinary player and does not open a failed film window (Hypnos commit `2203098`). This was a player-routing issue, separate from the later stale **stereo capture** lifecycle bug. An app crash during edge tests came from DebugTrace serializing a deep MetricKit call stack on a 512 KB thread, not stereo; it was fixed in DebugTrace commit `23e70c7`.

The prior session ended by recommending measured performance, an aspect-matched model, and a renderer that handles the uncovered strip more honestly. It had already learned that reducing the *video* resolution alone would not improve a depth model working at fixed input resolution, and that 21:9 mattered mainly because letterboxing the 518×392 depth input wasted vertical samples. The stronger content difference was close subjects against far open backgrounds versus room scenes. This led to the October 12–13 prototype and custom-model experiments below.

**October 12–13: the boundary renderer's own revisions.** The first opt-in prototype discarded every triangle crossing a sharp depth jump and filled holes horizontally from nearby background. A paused *Nope* screenshot showed black gaps along the horizontal railing: an entire row could have no background candidate. A vertical search removed those gaps. The next paused comparison showed jagged cap, face and rail edges because discarding the whole triangle also discarded valid foreground. The prototype was changed to draw near and far portions of crossing triangles separately. Matching point-sampled depth classification and colour sampling then stopped foreground texels from bleeding into the hole fill. The Metal tests passed after each revision, including thin rails, smooth slopes and both eyes. On the later real headset A/B, this revised version still produced noisy edges, especially the vertical railing. It remains opt-in; synthetic coverage only verifies its mechanism.

## Symptoms and distinct causes

- Near silhouettes, hair, sleeves, ears and railing could appear stretched. A single eye could look mostly acceptable while the two eyes together made the whole image uncomfortable. Reducing depth strength to 0.4× made it flatter but left patches, so strength reduction alone was not a satisfactory fix.
- The connected stereo mesh spans depth discontinuities. Its triangles can bridge a foreground subject and background. The original `meshEdgeFill` and `depthEdgeSnap` heuristics tried to hide this, but on the tested frames they also changed valid jacket and face contours.
- A separate, much larger distortion appeared with a custom 686×308 depth model. Vision returned a float16 `MLMultiArray` with shape `[1,1,308,686]` and strides `[216832,216832,704,1]`. Readers that assumed rows were packed at 686 elements shifted each row by 18 elements. This was a data-layout bug, not a property of a widescreen model or the stereo mesh. Vergence now honors reported row and column strides in live, cached/raw and still-depth reads (commit `b6008b1`). The headset confirmation was that the broad distortion disappeared.
- Closing the *Nope* film could leave its paused stereo pump alive. While *House* was the only open player, some captures still returned the old *Nope* frame. The pump treated an empty frame queue as a pause and retained `lastFrame`. The film now closes its frame source on stereo stop or scene disconnect; the pump observes permanent closure, stops its timer and drains its decoder/frame state. After deployment, `film` reported `stereoTap:false`, six fresh captures were all *House* at 1920×1080, and the user saw no visual issue in the reopened *House* scene.

## Experiments and decisions

The prototype remains available for research; it needs antialiasing, better reconstruction around uncertain depth, and device performance work. |
| Custom Depth Anything V2 Small 686×308, same rough pixel count as the default model | Exposed the row-stride bug. After the fix, large-scale distortion was gone, but residual sleeve/ear patches and low frame rate remained. Depth stage was roughly 40–53 ms. | Keep the stride fix. Do not assume an aspect-matched input or equal pixel count guarantees quality or ANE speed. |
| 0.4× depth strength on the paused *Nope* frame | Image became flatter; left-eye disturbances remained. | Keep full strength for subsequent controlled comparisons. |
| Custom Small 560×252 widescreen model | Export validated against PyTorch on the captured frame (correlation 1.0000, max difference 0.0176). User reported smoother playback and improvement with remaining jacket artifacts. Depth stage was roughly 25–34 ms; several continuous-play samples were about 21–24 fps. | Useful real-time option, but not an automatic default or a complete edge solution. The generated model is a local, ignored artifact, not bundled. |
| Disable original background edge fill | Exact paused *Nope* horse-rider frame at 233.149125 s; user said the left jacket edge improved. | `DepthRefinement.fillsUncoveredEdges=false` by default; retain the live debug toggle. |
| Disable luma-based outline snapping | Paused *House* frame at 670.317811 s; user said face and other subject edges improved. | `DepthRefinement.snapsEdges=false` by default; retain the live debug toggle. Joint-bilateral refinement and shot anchoring remain enabled. |
| Film source close and pump finish signal | After closing *Nope* and opening *House*, no stale *Nope* captures in six attempts. User reported no remaining visual issue. | Keep explicit closure; an empty queue must not mean a disconnected player can run forever. |

The `build/edge-comparison/` captures are local, ignored diagnostic artifacts. `nope-stride-fixed/manifest.json` is a valid same-frame original/prototype comparison (same source/depth hashes, timestamp and stereo settings). `nope-560-jacket/` is the exact paused edge-fill A/B. `house-face-targeted/` holds the snap A/B. **Do not use `nope-560-fixed/` as A/B evidence:** playback advanced during capture. The `scripts/compare-stereo-edges.py` helper saves both eyes, source, depth, anaglyph and a validation manifest, then restores switches. The `stereoCaptureInfo` endpoint provides timestamp, dimensions and disparity settings when making manual captures.

## Reproduce or continue the investigation

1. Pause the same shot and record the model, picture tier, source timestamp, disparity strength, convergence and current `stereoEdges` switches. Use the exact same decoded source and depth map for renderer A/B. A screenshot of one eye alone cannot settle stereo comfort.
2. Query `stereoEyes` for `source`, `depth`, `left` and `right`; use `stereoCaptureInfo` or run `python3 scripts/compare-stereo-edges.py --out build/edge-comparison/<name>`. Reject a comparison if its manifest is invalid or the player advances. Check both eyes on the headset, especially high-contrast vertical rails and soft hair/clothing edges.
3. Separate data-layout defects from geometric artifacts. For any new `MLMultiArray` shape, log/check `shape`, `strides` and dtype rather than deriving the physical row stride from width. The 686×308 model's 704-element physical row is the regression example.
4. Keep the boundary prototype opt-in until it beats the connected renderer on real paused frames *and* in motion. Its current single-sample rasterization and nearby-background fill do not reconstruct fine, translucent or wrongly estimated contours reliably.
5. For lifecycle regressions, close the film window before opening a different title, then confirm `film` has no stereo tap and repeated captures belong only to the current title. A paused player is valid; a closed source is terminal.

The local model export and device-copy commands, switch meanings, and test entry points are in the Hypnos and Vergence READMEs. The reported improvement applies to the tested headset scenes and settings; *Alpha*, other content, and automatic 16:9 versus cinema-aspect model selection still need validation.
