# Project the artwork onto a generated 360° body

As of November 2026, no single open model takes one piece of digital artwork and returns a wrap-around 3D asset whose visible side is as faithful as Apple's Spatial Scenes. The workable answer is a hybrid. An open 3D-native generator builds the full 360° geometry and a plausible back. Then the original image is projected back onto the surfaces it actually shows, and the two textures are blended at the seam. **TRELLIS.2 (Microsoft, MIT) and Pixal3D (Tsinghua + Tencent ARC, MIT code, built on TRELLIS.2 and designed for input-view alignment) are the two models to build on.** Both now run natively in ComfyUI with NVIDIA's non-commercial dependencies removed, and both fit the RTX 3090's 24 GB. Licences rule out much of the rest of the field for an EU developer shipping a paid app. **Every open Hunyuan3D release excludes the EU from its licensed territory.** The weights of Apple's SHARP and NVIDIA's Lyra 2.0 are research-only, and StdGEN, the best open anime-character model, ships research-only checkpoints. Blender renders are the easiest case, and much easier than flat illustrations. They come with exact alpha, extra views on demand and often the original mesh, so the projection can be exact and the back can be checked against ground truth. Flat, cel-shaded art is where these models fail: it produces abnormally thin geometry, and keylines break. On Vision Pro, a mesh exported as USDZ with a projected texture is the format that can be made pixel-exact today. RealityKit 27's native `GaussianSplatComponent` makes a splat route possible on visionOS 27, but only on a real device. On-device generation of quality 360° assets is not credible yet. The realistic path down the hardware ladder is a 16 GB GPU, then a 24 GB Mac, then a small feed-forward reconstructor on the device itself.

## Every open generator redraws the front instead of copying it

The gap the user has spotted is structural, not a matter of model size. Apple's SHARP regresses gaussians that are pixel-aligned to the input photo in under a second. It targets "nearby views" and makes no 360° claim ([apple/ml-sharp](https://github.com/apple/ml-sharp)). Splatter Image works the same way: one gaussian per input pixel reproduces the input view by construction, but the back comes only from category priors learned on ShapeNet- and CO3D-style data ([Splatter Image, CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Szymanowicz_Splatter_Image_Ultra-Fast_Single-View_3D_Reconstruction_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). Generators that do wrap around get there by synthesizing appearance rather than copying it. TRELLIS.2 generates PBR attributes inside its sparse-voxel latent and exports a GLB with base colour, roughness, metallic and opacity maps up to 4096² ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Hunyuan3D-Paint and Step1X-3D generate multi-view images conditioned on the input, then bake them. Hunyuan's stated goal is that the texture "should closely conform to the image prompt in the visible region", which is soft conditioning, not projection, and its delighting step deliberately changes the input's shading ([Hunyuan3D 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12202)). The character-specialised models move even further from the input. StdGEN and StdGEN++ first re-render the character into an A-pose and then build the mesh from that synthesized image. Their only fidelity measure is a CLIP similarity of about 0.94 ([StdGEN++](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07660v1); [StdGEN](https://github.com/hyz317/StdGEN)).

Pixal3D is the one open model that attacks this problem directly. Its authors trace the low fidelity of 3D-native generators to an "implicit 2D-3D correspondence issue". Their fix is a conditioner that lifts multi-scale image features into a 3D volume, so the asset is generated in the input view's alignment rather than in a canonical pose, and they claim fidelity "approaching the fidelity level of reconstruction" ([Pixal3D project](https://ldyang694.github.io/projects/pixal3d/)). That alignment matters twice. It is the fidelity win, and it also means the output is already registered to the input camera, which is the precondition for projecting the original pixels back onto it. The comparison evidence is visual only, against TRELLIS.2 and Hunyuan 3.1, whose meshes had to be rotated by hand to match the input. ComfyUI's own write-up adds that the authors still rate closed generators (Hunyuan 3D, Tripo, Rodin) higher and call their textures imperfect ([Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/trellis2-and-pixal3d-are-now-native)). A diffusion model working at 512 to 768 px through a VAE cannot return the input exactly. So even Pixal3D should be treated as the best geometry-and-back generator, not as the final front texture.

## Licences remove Hunyuan, SHARP and StdGEN before quality is even judged

For a paid app built by an EU developer, the licence filter decides more than quality does. Hunyuan3D 2.1's licence defines its Territory as worldwide "excluding the territory of the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea", and its preamble states it "DOES NOT APPLY" there. Use outside the Territory is "unlicensed and unauthorized". The same applies to 2.0, 2mv and Omni ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 LICENSE](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE); [Hunyuan3D-2mv LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2mv/blob/main/LICENSE)). This is not a shipping detail. Even running Hunyuan as a personal tool in the EU is outside the grant, and third-party claims that it is Apache-2.0 are simply wrong. The exclusion is painful, because Hunyuan's dense vecset architecture is the easiest to port to Apple Silicon (see below), and Hunyuan3D-Omni's point-cloud control is the only open route found that could consume a Blender depth pass. Both doors are closed. The newer Hunyuan 2.5, 3.0 and 3.1 have no open weights at all ([Cinevva timeline](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026)).

SHARP's weights are licensed "exclusively for Research Purposes", which excludes "use in any commercial product or service", and derivatives inherit that limit ([LICENSE_MODEL](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple/ml-sharp/main/LICENSE_MODEL)). So the tempting design of using SHARP's splats as the front shell of a generated asset is closed for a product. Convolution's existing use of Spatial Scenes through `ImagePresentationComponent` is a different matter, because that is an OS API and not the weights. But nothing found shows that RealityKit exposes those generated splats as an editable `GaussianSplatResource` ([radiancefields.com](https://radiancefields.com/platforms/apple)). Lyra 2.0 is under NVIDIA's internal research licence, which forbids commercial and production use ([nvidia/Lyra-2.0](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Lyra-2.0)). StdGEN's code is Apache-2.0, but its checkpoints are "for research purposes only" ([StdGEN](https://github.com/hyz317/StdGEN)), and the newer anime models (StdGEN++, Anime-Ready, SeparateGen) have no confirmed public code.

The clean stack is short but sufficient. TRELLIS and TRELLIS.2 are MIT for code and weights. The official TRELLIS.2 repo, however, depends on nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec under NVIDIA's own terms. ComfyUI's native integration of TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D (31 August 2026) removed those non-commercial dependencies and says the outputs are commercially usable ([Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/trellis2-and-pixal3d-are-now-native)). Stable3DGen did the same for Hi3DGen ([GitHub](https://github.com/Stable-X/Hi3DGen)). Step1X-3D, MV-Adapter, FreeSplatter and CharacterGen are Apache-2.0. For segmentation, BiRefNet, BEN2 and the anime fine-tune ToonOut are MIT, while Bria's RMBG-2.0 is CC BY-NC and should be avoided ([ToonOut](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06839v1); [RMBG-2.0](https://github.com/Bria-AI/RMBG-2.0)). Three items remain unverified before anything ships. Pixal3D's weight licence is not stated in its README. SAM 3D Objects' SAM License was not read from the primary text. Stability's SPAR3D and SF3D are free only under US$1M in annual revenue ([SPAR3D](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-point-aware-3d)).

## Stylized art breaks the models in predictable ways that Blender can avoid

The models are trained largely on Objaverse-style CG assets, so a clean Blender render with ordinary shading is close to their training distribution. Flat-colour art is not. Art3D documents that large image-to-3D models "struggle with flat-colored images because they lack a 3D illusion" and builds a Flat-2D benchmark around the problem ([Art3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10466)). REVIVE 3D (April 2026) adds Compactness and Normal Anisotropy metrics for the thin, flat geometry such inputs produce ([REVIVE 3D](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.27504)). The most detailed failure report is a vendor-side test, so it carries a conflict of interest. It ran one anime character through Meshy as a single image and as three views. With one image, black keylines thinned and broke into the cloth and painted hair highlights became hard streaks. Appliqués flattened, a two-tone hair split reverted to one colour, the ponytail collapsed, and the asymmetric leg warmers were made symmetric. With three views, those details came back for the surfaces the views showed ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/i-ran-the-same-anime-character-through-meshy-multiview-and-single-image-3d)). No controlled, quantitative single- versus multi-view comparison on stylized input exists for any open model. That is the largest evidence gap in this whole question, and it is why the test plan below measures it.

Character-specialised models do not fix this for the user's mix of content. The anime-capable family (CharacterGen, StdGEN, StdGEN++, NOVA-3D, Anime-Ready, SeparateGen) is trained on VRoid Hub avatars, so it learns "VRoid-shaped humanoid". It does model hair and clothing as separate layers, which is useful for rigging later ([StdGEN++](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07660v1)). The human-reconstruction line (PSHuman, LHM, AniGS, SAM 3D Body) is trained and evaluated on real people. PSHuman also needs more than 40 GB at 768 px ([PSHuman](https://github.com/pengHTYX/PSHuman)). Weapons, wings, tails, mascots and props are out of distribution for all of them. For the general case of stylized characters and objects, a general object generator is the safer default, with character models kept as a reference to compare against.

Blender changes the problem from inference to measurement. It supplies exact alpha, so thin hair strands are not softened by a matting model. It can re-render the same subject with plain Lambert or soft lighting, which gives back the shading cues that flat toon materials remove. It can switch off the Freestyle outline pass, so keylines are not baked in and broken, and outlines can be added back in RealityKit with an inverted hull or a shader (an untested inference). Most importantly, it can render any number of consistent views. Pixal3D's multiview workflow takes a front, side and back turnaround sheet and "uses the extra viewpoints to reconstruct geometry the single-image pipeline cannot see". The docs ask for consistent scale and pose ([ComfyUI Pixal3D docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/3d/pixal3d)). TRELLIS v1 has a training-free multi-image mode, with a warning that results vary ([TRELLIS](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS)). When the original mesh exists, the strongest option is to skip generation for the visible side: camera-project the render onto the real mesh in Blender, and use TRELLIS.2's "texture a given shape" pipeline or MV-Adapter's ig2mv mode only for texels no render covers ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [MV-Adapter](https://github.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter)). No open generator accepts a rendered normal or depth pass directly. Hi3DGen feeds on normals it estimates itself, so substituting a Blender normal pass would mean changing code to match its camera-space, white-background convention ([Hi3DGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22236v2)).

## The exact front comes from a projection bake that no tool ships

No surveyed tool guarantees input-view pixels on a 360° asset, so the composite has to be built. It has four steps. (1) Generate geometry and a full texture with Pixal3D, which is view-aligned, or TRELLIS.2, which needs pose registration by silhouette alignment. (2) Project the original image onto the texels that are front-facing and pass a depth test from the input camera. (3) Feather the projected texture into the generated one by the cosine of the view angle, so the transition happens near grazing angles rather than as a hard line. (4) Blend colour at the seam. The research supports the concept and warns about the details. One community Hunyuan wrapper already projects each view onto the surfaces its camera sees, with depth testing and blending ([HartsyInference PR #933](https://github.com/HartsyAI/HartsyInference/pull/933)). A CVPR 2026 review notes that reprojection methods suffer "inconsistencies and occlusion blind spots" ([MV2UV notes](https://github.com/zhaoyang97/Paper-Notes-en/blob/main/docs/CVPR2026/3d_vision/mv2uv_generating_high-quality_uv_texture_maps_with_multiview_prompts.md)), and another paper finds that baking can misalign pixels when camera parameters are slightly off ([arXiv 2505.21050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21050)). For stylized art, the seam is the hard part. The front keeps its painted shading and outlines, while the generated sides are shaded by the model's own prior. A delighting step would remove style that the artist intended, so it should be skipped for CG input.

A splat variant of the same idea is simpler to make exact. Train or fine-tune a 3DGS asset with the ordinary photometric loss: weight the input view as a hard target, and use views rendered from the generated mesh as low-weight supervision for the rest. That is standard weighted 3DGS training, not a new method. The result keeps SHARP's soft, view-dependent look without SHARP's weights. A 2026 TVCG method for propagating a single edited view's texture onto an existing 3DGS points the same way ([TVCG 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/tvcg.2026.3684949)). Pose-free sparse-view splatters (FreeSplatter, Apache-2.0; AnySplat) reconstruct only what the views show. They are useful when Blender supplies 4 to 8 views, but they leave the unseen regions empty, which defeats the point for single images ([FreeSplatter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09573)).

On Vision Pro, the two formats behave differently. RealityKit 27 adds `GaussianSplatComponent`, fed by format-agnostic `GaussianSplatResource.BufferResource` buffers (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH degree 0–3). It ships on visionOS, iOS and macOS 27, can load standard 3DGS PLY fields with their activations, and can load USD `ParticleField3DGaussianSplat` prims. It does not render in Simulator. Splats ignore scene lighting, and the budget is unpublished: RealityKit lowers quality on its own and posts `GaussianSplatEvents.RenderingChanged` when an asset costs too much ([WWDC26 session 279](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/279/); [Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionos/rendering-gaussian-splats-with-realitykit.md)). OpenUSD 26.03's `UsdVolParticleField3DGaussianSplat` means splats can live in USDZ ([radiancefields.com](https://radiancefields.com/platforms/apple)). On visionOS 26, MetalSplatter 1.0 renders PLY and SPZ in stereo through Metal ([MetalSplatter](https://github.com/scier/MetalSplatter)). A USDZ mesh with the projected texture is the most robust first target. It is lit by the scene, easy to make pixel-exact, and goes through the portfolio's own AnythingUSDZ glTF converter. One trap: TRELLIS.2 exports its GLB as OPAQUE by default even when the texture carries alpha, so hair cards and transparent parts must be rewired ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). For CG art whose shading is part of the style, unlit splats are arguably an advantage, not a defect.

## The 3090 runs the whole stack, and the path down the ladder is clear

The RTX 3090 meets TRELLIS.2's official 24 GB floor. NVIDIA's README verifies only A100 and H100, and H100 timings are about 17 s at 1024³ and 60 s at 1536³, so the 3090 will be slower, plausibly tens of seconds to around a minute, which is not yet measured ([TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Pixal3D inherits that stack and offers `--low_vram` at 1024 resolution. In every pipeline the real memory peak is the texture stage, not the DiT. Hunyuan 2.1 needs 10 GB for shape but 21 GB for paint, and TripoSG's texture step runs a 24 GB card out of memory while its shape step fits in 8 GB ([low-end notes: Hunyuan3D-2.1](https://gh.ali360.cloudns.ch/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1); [TripoSG discussion](https://huggingface.co/spaces/VAST-AI/TripoSG/discussions/6)). The standard pattern is to load stages one after another, never together. The same rule governs every step down.

The RTX 5080 (16 GB, Blackwell sm_120) brings a specific trap. CuMesh's `remesh_narrow_band_dc` produces fragmented, non-watertight meshes (58,265 components in one report) on every RTX 50-series card, and recompiling for sm_120 does not fix it. The issue was open with no fix as of the page read, and the workaround is a CPU marching-cubes path ([CuMesh #27](https://github.com/JeffreyXiang/CuMesh/issues/27)). Blackwell also needs cu128+ PyTorch, `TORCH_CUDA_ARCH_LIST=12.0` source builds, and community spconv wheels ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/no-kernel-image-available-rtx-50); [spconv-wheels](https://github.com/fafraob/spconv-wheels)). Generate on the 3090 and treat the 5080 as a port target. Below 16 GB, the evidence is community-only. A TRELLIS.2 GGUF Q4 build cuts weights to about 2.5 GB from 8 GB in BF16 ([trellis-2-rocm write-up](https://github.com/toastmanAu/trellis-2-rocm-comfyui)), and a 12 GB RTX 4070 Super user reported about 30 minutes per GLB ([Godot forums](https://godotforums.org/d/43783-img-to-3d-locally-6gb-vram-nvida)). TripoSG (MIT) gives geometry only in about 7.4 GB on an 8 GB card ([Reddit](https://redlib.cypherpunktools.com/r/StableDiffusion/comments/1jpl4tm/open_sourcing_triposg_highfidelity_3d_generation/ml08a33/?context=3)). Texture under 10 GB means MV-Adapter's SD2.1 variant or vertex colours, at a visible quality cost.

Apple Silicon works now, but slowly. The trellis-mac port replaces every CUDA extension (sparse conv with `mtlgemm`, flash-attn with SDPA, nvdiffrast with `mtldiffrast`, CuMesh skipped). It runs TRELLIS.2 at 512 in 3 min 20 s warm on an M4 Pro with 24 GB, peaking at about 18 GB, with hole filling still disabled ([trellis-mac](https://github.com/shivampkumar/trellis-mac)). Distillation is the lever for the long term: MDT-dist cuts TRELLIS v1's flow transformers to one or two steps, 0.68 to 0.94 s on an A800 ([MDT-dist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04406)). No such distillation exists yet for TRELLIS.2, and whether the MDT-dist weights are public was not verified. On phones and headsets, the only demonstrated models are small feed-forward reconstructors. SF3D runs on iPhone and iPad through ONNX Runtime with vertex colours ([sf3d-ios](https://huggingface.co/jc-builds/sf3d-ios)), and TripoSR takes about 15 s on a phone CPU ([Arm blog](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/can-mobile-phones-generate-a-3d-object-at-game-runtime-in-unity-here-is-what-actually-happens)). Neither is a quality wrap-around generator. The memory arithmetic, by contrast, is no longer the blocker for TRELLIS.2 on an M5: 4B parameters at Q4 is about 2.5 GB. Compute and Metal kernel coverage (fused sparse attention, mesh ops) are. For the foreseeable future, generation lives on a GPU server or a Mac, and the headset shows the result.

## Recommendations and the ordered test plan for the 3090

Build the prototype on **ComfyUI-native Pixal3D, with ComfyUI-native TRELLIS.2 as the comparison baseline**. Run it as a single-worker queue on the 3090 behind ComfyUI's `/prompt` API, with models kept warm and stages loaded one at a time. Write the front projection bake yourself in Blender's Python API, since no tool ships it. Ship USDZ meshes first and treat RealityKit 27 splats as the second format. Keep Hunyuan3D, SHARP weights, Lyra 2.0, StdGEN checkpoints and RMBG-2.0 out of the pipeline entirely, including for experiments whose outputs might feed the product. Before relying on Pixal3D, confirm its weight licence on Hugging Face. One operational note: the RTX 3090 is the `ai` host in the bjorkbox group. One-off test runs there are ordinary use, but a standing generation service on that box is the kind of thing to decide on deliberately rather than assume. No app code should be written until steps 1 to 6 below have produced numbers.

| # | Test on the 3090 | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Build a 12–16 item test set** with ground truth: Blender characters and props rendered with alpha at the input camera, plus held-out side, back and three-quarter renders; each in toon/Freestyle and Lambert/no-outline variants; a turnaround (front/left/back/right) per subject; add 3–4 real illustrations without a mesh (anime, painterly), one non-humanoid creature and one weapon or prop. | Gives the measurable baseline that no source provides: back-side accuracy against a real back. |
| 2 | **Single-image baseline**: TRELLIS.2 at 1024³ and 1536³, Pixal3D at default 1536 and `--low_vram` 1024. Log peak VRAM (`nvidia-smi --query-gpu=memory.used -lms 200`), wall time, front-view LPIPS/PSNR rendered at the input camera, held-out-view LPIPS, and silhouette IoU. | Which generator leads, and whether Pixal3D's alignment shows up in numbers on stylized input. |
| 3 | **Preprocessing ablation** on the winner: render alpha vs ToonOut vs BiRefNet-HR mattes; toon vs Lambert re-render; outlines on vs off; check thickness (Compactness-style) on flat art. | How much Blender's control buys, and what an illustration-only user must do. |
| 4 | **Multi-view gain**: Pixal3D multiview and TRELLIS v1 multi-image with 2, 3 and 4 Blender views vs single image, on the same metrics. | Whether to offer a turnaround input mode, and how many views it needs. |
| 5 | **Projection bake prototype** in Blender: import the GLB, register it to the input camera (direct for Pixal3D, silhouette-fit for TRELLIS.2), project the input onto depth-tested front texels, feather by view-angle cosine; measure front LPIPS against the input (target: near zero) and inspect the seam at ±60–90°. | Whether the hybrid gets SHARP-class front fidelity, and how bad the seam is on cel-shaded art. |
| 6 | **Own-mesh path** for Blender subjects: camera-project the render onto the real mesh, then fill unseen texels with TRELLIS.2 texturing and MV-Adapter ig2mv (SDXL); compare with step 5. | Whether Blender users should skip generation entirely. |
| 7 | **Splat route**: train a 3DGS from orbit renders of the step-5 asset, with the input view weighted as a hard target; export PLY/SPZ; on a device running visionOS 27, load it with `GaussianSplatComponent` (not Simulator) and watch `RenderingChanged` while varying splat count. | Splat budget per asset and whether unlit splats beat the lit mesh for stylized art. |
| 8 | **Vision Pro mesh check**: convert the step-5 GLB to USDZ via AnythingUSDZ, fix the OPAQUE alpha mode, view it in RealityKit at 1:1 scale, and compare it side by side with the Spatial Scene of the same image. | The actual quality gap the user sees on the headset. |
| 9 | **Low-VRAM simulation** on the 3090: cap memory with `torch.cuda.set_per_process_memory_fraction` at 16/12/8 GB; try TRELLIS.2 GGUF Q4/Q8 and Pixal3D `--low_vram`, sequential stage loading, MV-Adapter SD2.1 texturing; record the quality drop against step 2. | Which tier the 5080 and smaller cards can serve, before buying or porting anything. |

## Conclusion

The question is not which model to wait for. It is which half of the problem a model should own. 3D-native generators are now good and permissively licensed enough to own geometry and the hidden sides. The visible side should stay a projection of the artist's pixels, because every generative path, Pixal3D included, re-draws them. That split also eases the hardware problem. The expensive, model-heavy part (geometry plus a rough full texture) tolerates the quality loss of quantization and small texture models far better than the front would, because the front's fidelity comes from a cheap bake, not from the model. That makes a 16 GB, and later a Mac, tier credible without matching the 3090's output pixel for pixel.

Two things would change this plan, and both are worth watching. One is a confirmed commercial licence for Pixal3D's weights, or the lack of one.
