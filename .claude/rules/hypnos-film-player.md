# Hypnos: film player, object audio, Dolby Vision and picture tiers

Moved out of `CLAUDE.md`.

## Shared player controls and Atmos films

`RAVEUI.RAVEPlayerControls` owns the custom transport UI shared with Raven. App adapters supply playback state, commands and feature actions; the control never owns a decoder or an audio session. The platform-native AVKit players on Mac/tvOS and Raven's cinema player retain their system transport and media-selection UI.

`FilmPlayerView` uses those same custom controls on every platform: a picture with a separate Sound and Picture window on visionOS (a sheet elsewhere), mute, and Recenter Audio while iOS/macOS AirPods tracking is active (tvOS uses its PHASE recenter request). `FilmPlayer.isMuted` zeros render gains independently of the saved gain tuning. visionOS offers realtime 3D via `FilmVideoPlayer.onSampleBuffer` / `onFlush` into RAVEMedia's `SampleBufferFrameSource`, which decodes BGRA on the pump queue, selects pictures by the film timebase and resets at seeks. It does not open another AVPlayer or move audio to another clock. The normal picture renderer remains mounted; the stereo tap adds a second video decode session. Both film picture paths reserve 180pt for the ornament in a plain window and retains the measured 90pt recess. Decode failures return to 2D; model installation uses the existing setup sheet. The visualizer lives only in adjustments: visionOS/macOS have scrolling controls on the left and a pinned object map on the right. B-frame selection/reset tests run in RAVEMediaTests on macOS. Device confirmation of the new film stereo layout, HDR appearance, decode cost and Atmos synchronization remains required.

**Speed** (the Playback items' `PlaybackSpeedMenu`, every platform): `FilmPlayer.setSpeed` re-anchors both halves at the playhead, the picture's timebase runs at the speed and the object audio is time-stretched at its own pitch by RAVEFilm's `FilmTimeStretch` (WSOLA on the output timeline; each 30 ms grain's splice is chosen once, from a downmix without the LFE, and shared by every element's callback, so the objects stay sample-locked; never more than 12 ms from the picture). The room reverb is fed the stretched mix. The speed lasts while the player is open (the next episode too) and is back to 1× when it closes. Above 1× real-time AI 3D is held to the film's 1× frame rate and the overload step-down still applies (see the video window's Playback speed note). Not yet heard on a headset or measured there: the stretch's sound on real mixes and its cost with 100+ channel scenes.

**The room** (Sound and Picture → Room, `FilmRoomPicker`): RealityKit stages (visionOS, iOS, macOS) offer the system's acoustics ("Your Room" on visionOS) or RAVEFilm's own `FilmRoomReverb` (same preset names as tvOS's PHASE rooms, plus Open Air = none). visionOS ignores `ReverbComponent` in a window or `mixed` space (Apple's docs: it applies only while a `progressive`/`full` space is open) and always reverberates spatial sources with a simulation of the wearer's real room. So with a preset chosen, each object's system send drops to −120 dB and the reverb plays as four dry sources around the listener. A windowed app has no API telling it about a system Environment or another app's space (another app's full space just backgrounds our windows), which is why there is no automatic mode yet. Not yet heard on a headset: whether a −120 dB send really leaves the objects dry.

**Where the listener is** (visionOS, `FilmStageView`): the object room is centred a guessed distance in front of the player window, because a window can't see the head (ARKit's head pose needs an open immersive space). A window pushed away grows to keep its angular size, and a `RealityView`'s metres grow with it (`WorldScalingCompensation`), so the stage reads the scale (unscaled ÷ scaled `physicalMetrics`) and shrinks the room by it: room sizes are real metres at any window distance. "Follow the window's distance" (default, `FilmListenerDistance.automatic`) puts the listener 1.5 m away at the window's natural size and proportionally further as its scale grows; off, a slider sets real metres (0.3–12). The `film` debug query reports `windowScale` and `listenerDistanceMetres`. Not yet measured on a headset: which distance a window is at its natural size (the 1.5 m is the old guess), and whether visionOS's scaling stays proportional out to 5–10 m. Push the window to known distances and compare `windowScale` against them.

**Cinema** (`Views/Cinema/Cinema.swift`; a Cinema button pinned to the control strip of the film player *and* every video window, kept on the bar like the volume button via `RAVEPlayerControls(pinned:)`): a mixed immersive space of our own, since system environment docking (Apple's screen over the lake in Mount Hood) only takes an `AVPlayerViewController`. One player owns it at a time (`CinemaOwner`, the last to choose a mode); the one it took over from goes back to its window.
- **Room Sound** (film only) moves the stage into the space and centres it on the head (ARKit's device anchor, which only an open space gets), facing the film window, whose pose an entity in it reads with `transformMatrix(relativeTo: .immersiveSpace)`.
- **Big Screen** puts the picture on a screen `screenDistance` (2–80 m, default 15) ahead and `screenWidthDegrees` (default 55°) wide, which is how a picture gets further and larger than a window may; the HUD's grab bar hangs under it, scaled with the distance, and drags it the way visionOS drags a window (`Cinema.dragScreen`: across in proportion to the distance from a hand at ~0.5 m, depth exponential at e⁴ per metre pushed, keeping the screen's angular size and facing the head); surroundings dim (`.systemDark`) and the window shows a card with Place in Front of Me and Back to Window. The film moves `videoEntity` (same component and renderer, nothing reloads). A video window lends its native renderer's `AVPlayer` (`VideoWindowModel.nativePlayer`) to a `VideoPlayerComponent(avPlayer:)` there and hides its own picture without unmounting it; a WebKit (WebM) source switches to the server transcode first, and without one Big Screen says it can't. Live 3D and AI 3D come along (since 2026-11-01): the window lends its stereo engine (`VideoWindowModel.stereoEngine`, `Cinema.setScreenStereo`), whose plane (`Pseudo3DStereoEngine.lendPicture`/`reclaimPicture`, same renderer, nothing reloads) moves onto the screen; while lent the engine leaves its fit, fade and tap target alone, and AI 3D's strength follows the screen's width. Subtitles drawn into the stereo picture stay there. The immersive MV-HEVC player ends Big Screen and it ends that. Not yet tried on a headset; its sound still comes from the window in HUD mode (`routeAudio` only knows the flat player). Subtitles are drawn on the screen (the owner's `SubtitleController` in a `ViewAttachmentComponent` scaled from its measured bounds). **The next episode keeps Big Screen:** the window hides its picture for as long as Big Screen is chosen (`Cinema.hidesPicture`), the screen keeps the last picture until the new player is lent, and `VideoWindowModel.holdsBigScreen` stops the new episode auto-starting AI 3D (a stereo episode still plays in 3D there) and sends a WebM episode straight to the transcode instead of mounting WebKit.
- **Head-Locked Sound** (one setting for all players): a video window's 5.1/7.1 becomes `.fixed` spatial audio through `RAVEMediaPolicy.headLockedSurround`, which every playing multichannel `AVPlayer` the policy saw follows live. Custom-rendered video players anchor stereo and surround to their owning `UIScene` so two open windows don't share the first window's sound position; consumers without a scene ID keep the existing stereo-spatialization policy. The film's object room takes the head's full pose each frame, which needs the space, so a head-locked film opens it even in Window mode (and on opening).

**The Cinema button, and every choice on the bar, is a popover, not a Menu** (`PlayerChoiceMenu`: view mode, quality, subtitles, audio, speed; a tvOS Menu only), like the bar's More: a Menu whose choice opens or closes the space never reported closing, and the player went on treating a menu as open, with the picture dimmed and deaf to taps (device, 2026-10-20). The bar's choices were Menus until 2026-10-23, when tapping Quality a second time to dismiss it never reported closing and left the picture dimmed, and every row of a Menu drew a checkmark (a hidden icon still draws in a system menu): `PlayerChoiceRow` is the one row type, and no row draws its own checkmark. A popover's binding is exact, a tap on the picture closes it (`menuDismissals`), and both players count it with More as a popover that leaves the picture tappable (`VideoWindowModel.popoverMenuNames`, which lists the bar's choices too; the host bumps `menuDismissals` into the `playerChoiceDismissal` environment value to close them; the film's named `chromeHolds`, which replaced a counter that a lost close left off by one).

**HUD menus use view attachments, not modal presentations.** visionOS rejects both SwiftUI popovers and `PresentationComponent` inside the immersive space ("Presentations are not currently supported in Immersive contexts", device, 2026-10-30). `ravePlayerPopover` routes the shared volume/More buttons and the app's choices/details through `RAVEImmersivePlayerPresentation`; `CinemaSpaceView` draws its content in a separate `ViewAttachmentComponent`, with a bounded 360pt width (More's expanding rows otherwise break the layout). The source button's frame in the HUD controls' named coordinate space places the menu above that button at the controls' physical scale. Nested choices keep their parent mounted and return to it on close; replacing a bar menu or tapping the picture clears the owning bindings. The user confirmed working menus and button alignment on the headset.

**HUD depth and recovery** (device-confirmed, 2026-10-31; reframed 2026-10-31, see the next paragraph): the grab bar moves in depth as well as across the view. Both Recenter and the original window's Place in Front of Me restore 2 m and the original 55° picture width. There is no distance slider: the first slider experiment allowed an unusably close picture.

**HUD framing keeps the picture in view** (`HUDFraming.swift`, pure maths, no framework): the HUD may come as near as 0.5 m (was 1.5) to 6 m, and three bounds stop the picture leaving the view. **Size:** `Cinema.pictureWidth` is `hudWidth` capped to 70° wide × 42° tall as seen from the head, so the picture keeps its physical size down to about 1.5 m and below that keeps its angular size, shrinking instead of overflowing (the edge-resize starts from the drawn width, since `hudWidth` can be larger than what is drawn). **Placement:** `moveHUD` and a per-frame `refitHUD` (also after a resize or a new aspect) hold the picture's centre where its whole extent stays inside the usable field of view, 50° × 40° half-angles with a 6° reserve, so the grab bar can no longer drag it to the side, above or behind; the controls and grab bar hanging below the picture are not counted. **Smoothing:** `CinemaHUDFollow.step` clamps the eased head to the real one by `HUDFraming.lagLimits`: what the picture leaves free of the view (never under 2°), 60% as a turn and 40% as a move at the picture's distance (13.5° and 31 cm for the default picture, less for a bigger or off-centre one), so smoothing still removes jitter and a step's bob but a fast turn can't leave the picture behind. The field of view numbers are a conservative guess, not measured: the headset's is wider. The maths is checked off-device (clamp, size cap, lag limits and their quaternion handling); **not yet tried on a headset:** how a 0.5 m picture and the controls under it feel, whether the bounds feel too tight, and the lag limits during fast turns and walking.

**HUD follow** (device-confirmed, 2026-10-31): the HUD hangs from `CinemaHUDFollow.head`, a world-space entity that every `SceneEvents.Update` eases toward the ARKit device anchor (horizontal 0.08 s, vertical 0.2 s, rotation 0.15 s), not from `AnchorEntity(.head)`, whose rigid lock shook the picture with every step (predicted head anchoring and frame-event layout didn't help). The head anchor holds the screen only until the follow's first pose; the drag converts into whatever the screen hangs from. The follow is a singleton that outlives the space, so `attach(to:keeping:)` drops every screen but the current one: each reopened space had left its `cinema-screen` on it (four were found), showing as a second, immovable picture.

**A real object nearer than the picture fades it, and nothing app-side stops it** (device, 2026-10-31). When a floor lamp or a wall stands between the wearer and immersive content, visionOS draws that content with a soft round vignette that turns its edges see-through, refreshes about once a second, and goes opaque again after a while of looking at it. It is system behaviour for the whole mixed space: Big Screen gets it too, and so did a plain `UnlitMaterial` plane, a `VideoMaterial` plane, a picture without subtitles or HUD chrome, a HUD frozen in the room, one pushed out to 15 m at the same angular size, and a `.dim` surroundings effect. `EnvironmentBlendingComponent` is opt-in occlusion whose `.default` is already "no blending". Accepted as a platform limit; don't re-chase it with depth, parenting, materials or surroundings effects. A Metal/CompositorServices layer was not tried (it would replace the RealityKit scene and its SwiftUI attachments, and likely goes through the same compositor).

**The HUD keeps `VideoPlayerComponent`.** For a video window, a `VideoMaterial(avPlayer:)` plane drops the system's grounding shadow, but it has no documented HDR, stereo (MV-HEVC) or comfort-mitigation support and doesn't help the vignette, so the component stays (user's call). The film's picture can't swap at all: setting `VideoMaterial(videoRenderer:)` or a new `VideoPlayerComponent(videoRenderer:)` on `FilmVideoPlayer`'s renderer while it enqueues crashes the app (`addVideoTarget while enqueueing`, device, 2026-10-31).

Every immersive space has `immersiveEnvironmentBehavior(.coexist)`, so a system environment stays up. One space at a time: Cinema closes Spatial 3D or the immersive video player first, and they can't open while Cinema is. The `filmCinema` debug query gives owner, mode, head lock, head/window/room/screen positions and distances. **Not yet tried on a headset:** all of it, in particular that a `VideoPlayerComponent` keeps drawing as its entity moves between a window's and the space's RealityView, that an `AVPlayer` shows in a `VideoPlayerComponent` while its window's layer and video output stay attached, where that component puts the video's sound, that an environment opened after the space stays, the screen's tap reaching the window's controls, and how the stage's swap between window and space sounds (generators are rebuilt). A progressive/full space would also bring `ReverbComponent`.

## Object audio over cellular and slow links

The film player fetches object audio as the plugin's lossless FLAC groups (~3 Mbps for a dozen elements) or, per segment, as one compressed bundle with a mono AAC stream per channel (`Segments/{n}/Bundle`, format in `JellyfinPlugin/README.md` → Compressed bundles; client `RAVEFilm.FilmAudioBundle`). **The plugin shares the bits unevenly** (`BundlePlanner`): `bitrate` is the average per audible channel, the LFE gets a third of it, the front-centre element (where dialogue is; found by position, because an Atmos track's only bed is usually the LFE) 1.5× and the rest of the front stage 1.25×, quiet channels fall to 0.45×, and element gain counts toward every level. A request's `budget` caps the whole segment (containers included), past which the quietest channels are left out. Measured, that is 0.1–2 dB better mix-level SNR than a flat rate at the same bytes, never worse; the one thing it must not do is delay a channel (an LFE low-pass did, by 1.5 ms: check lags, not just SNR).

`ObjectAudioQuality` (`Services/`, Audio Quality in the film player's Playback items): **Auto** is `RAVEFilm.FilmAudioGovernor`, one for the app (`ObjectAudioQuality.autoGovernor`). It measures every large transfer from the server (the picture's segments too) first byte to last, picks the best tier whose audio fits 40% of that (lossless, then AAC 96/64/48/32/24/16 kbps), steps down at once on a slow fetch, a failure or a stall caused by the link and back up only after 20 s and a 1.3× margin, sends a `budget` when even 16 kbps per channel won't fit, and never exceeds what the path allows (lossless unmetered, 64 kbps expensive, 32 kbps Low Data Mode; `NetworkCost`, which also bumps a generation when the path changes interface, so what Wi-Fi measured isn't believed on cellular). Nothing measured yet means a first segment at 96 kbps, then whatever the first measurement says. Tier changes need no reload (every tier decodes to the same PCM); `FilmPlayerView` says so for a few seconds when Auto steps down (`audioNotice`, beside the skip button), and the menu's Auto row shows where it is. Lossless and High/Standard/Low (96/64/32 kbps) are fixed, and changing the choice reloads the sound at the playhead (`FilmPlayer.setAudioEncoding(_:governor:)`). Downloads always play their FLAC. Opus bundles exist for non-Apple clients (a Quest, `FilmAudioGovernor(codec: .opus)`); Apple platforms use AAC.

The streamer fetches compressed segments up to 8 ahead of its decoded slots, as bytes (`FilmStreamingAudioSource`): lossless FLAC groups are fetched concurrently rather than one round trip each, and audio requests have a 90 s ceiling (the plugin's own cold-decode wait is 45 s, so its 404 comes first). The **video** is the plugin's untranscoded remux (86 Mbps for the 4K demo) unless a lower picture tier is chosen (below); Auto steps the picture only for 3D, not for the link, so on cellular pick 1080p by hand. `-LibraryHarness` checks bundle size, SNR, alignment (lags, per channel), joins, Auto on four paths and fetching ahead; `RAVEFilmLab` takes `FILM_AUDIO=aac:64000|auto FILM_LINK=cellular FILM_MUTE=1` for an end-to-end run, and `dotnet run` in `JellyfinPlugin/BundlePlannerCheck` checks the planner.

## Dolby Vision: the OS renders it, the server converts it

**Rule (2026-10-10, narrowed 2026-11-01): an App Store build never does Dolby processing itself.** On the device only the OS codecs and renderers read a Dolby Vision RPU (or decode TrueHD); when a picture needs converting, the server does it. The Infuse approach, for licensing reasons in a paid App Store app. **The sideload (GitHub) edition is exempt for audio:** it may decode TrueHD (its channel presentation; Atmos objects still come from the plugin) and DTS on the device with ffmpeg's LGPL decoders, behind a compilation condition that a submission build never sets, like `HYPNOS_YOUTUBE`. The Dolby Vision part of the rule still holds everywhere.
- **Profiles 8.1 and 10, and 7's HDR10 base** decode to correct HDR10 frames anywhere, so AI 3D, live 3D and the Metal player use them as is.
- **Profile 5** (no HDR10 base; most streaming WEB-DLs) decodes to purple and green IPT anywhere the app reads frames: AVFoundation attaches the RPU (`DolbyVisionRPUData`) but never applies it, with or without a video composition (measured on the Mac). Only the system's renderers show it right: the flat player's `AVPlayerLayer`, the film player's display layer, tvOS/macOS AVKit.
- So for 3D a profile 5 film plays the plugin's converted tier (below): `FilmPlayerView.startStereo` switches to it (1080, `FilmPictureQuality.stepDown`) and leaving 3D goes back to the original. A download of a profile 5 film keeps the converted 1080p HDR10 tier instead of the original (its stored index says HDR10), so 3D works offline. Elsewhere (video window: local files, Stash, Jellyfin titles without object audio) Vergence's `StereoPump` spots the first profile 5 frame (`DolbyVisionFrames.needsSystemRenderer`), the engine reports `Pseudo3DFailure.needsSystemRenderer`, and the window plays 2D, says why and stops offering 3D for that video. **Next step there:** serve the plugin's converted picture to the video window too.
- **Jellyfin keeps Dolby Vision only when asked**: `hevc-rangetype` (`JellyfinLibrary.hevcRangeTypes`) on the HLS and download remux URLs. Without it a copied stream loses DV (8.1 came back as plain HDR10). The enhancement-layer types are left out, so profile 7 is served as its HDR10 base. Jellyfin 12.2 writes 8.1 as `hvc1` with `dvcC`; 10.11 wrote `dvh1`.
- **Jellyfin's own transcoder can't make HDR10 from profile 5**: it tone-maps Dolby Vision to SDR only, and its VideoToolbox path skips profile 5 (`EncodingHelper.IsVideoToolboxTonemapAvailable`). That is why the conversion lives in the plugin.

## Film picture tiers

The plugin offers an object-audio film's picture below its own size as HEVC tiers cut on the same keyframe index (a Dolby Vision profile 5 film's tiers are converted to HDR10 by jellyfin-ffmpeg's `tonemap_videotoolbox`/`tonemapx` with `apply_dovi`, see the Dolby Vision section) (`JellyfinPlugin/README.md` → Picture tiers; `?height=1440|1080` on `Video/Init` and `Video/Segments/{n}`, listed in the index's `pictureHeights`), so segment n of every tier covers the same film time. `FilmPictureQuality` (`Services/`, `filmPlayer.pictureQuality`): **Auto** (visionOS only; elsewhere it reads as Original), **Original**, and whichever of **1440p**/**1080p** the film offers, chosen from the transport's Picture Quality menu (`FilmPictureQualityMenu`, beside Audio Quality) or the Picture section of Sound and Picture (`FilmPictureQualityPicker`; tvOS's `TVFilmSoundSettings` too), hidden when the film offers none. A fixed choice opens the film at that tier (`FilmPlayer.load(_:picture:startAt:pictureHeight:)`) and a change applies at the playhead (`FilmVideoPlayer.setPictureHeight`, which never touches the audio-led clock). Auto tries AI 3D at the current picture quality with a bounded depth reserve first; `FilmPlayerView.noteStereoPerformance` can still step an oversized 3D picture straight to 1080p (else the smallest lower tier) after three overloaded 3D reports (then 20 s of grace), for that film only, saying so in the `pictureNotice` slot ("Picture lowered to 1080p so 3D plays smoothly"); at the lowest tier, or with none, it gives the old advice once. Downloads play their original (the offline client offers and asks for no tier). On the Docker dev instance (libx265 in Linux) a tier encodes at about 0.3× realtime, too slow to watch; on the native one (`scripts/dev-jellyfin.sh native up`, VideoToolbox) ten cold 1 s 1080 segments arrive in 7 s on an M-series Mac. On servo (M1 Mac mini, Jellyfin's own ffmpeg) Baby Driver's 1080 tier (1920×804, PQ) serves 16 cold 1 s segments in 6 s, the first in 2.1 s; the headset step is not yet measured.
