# Hypnos: Library tab, Jellyfin, subtitles and offline downloads

Moved out of `CLAUDE.md`.

## Streaming quality (Library titles)

`LibraryStreamQuality` (`Services/Library/LibraryStreamQuality.swift`): **Auto** (default: original, i.e. direct play or a full-quality remux, stepped down when the stream can't keep up), **Always Direct Play** (original, never stepped) and six bitrate caps (40/30/20/10/4/1.5 Mbps: 4K, 1440p, 1080p twice, 720p, 480p; H.264 HLS with `MaxWidth`/`MaxHeight`; a cap at or above the file's own bitrate plays the original). **An HDR source asks for HEVC first** (`hlsStreamURL(sourceIsHDR:)`, from PlaybackInfo's `VideoRangeType`; capped levels, the AI 3D tier and non-HEVC HDR sources such as AV1), with the HEVC range types, since H.264 is always tone-mapped. Keeping HDR is the server's: on Jellyfin 12.2 (both dev instances, 2026-11-04, Dolby Vision 8.1 demo at 10 Mbps) HEVC encoding off (the default) or libx265 tone-maps to SDR (`tonemapx` even with tone mapping off), and VideoToolbox keeps PQ/BT.2020 but encodes 8-bit Main (`scale_vt format=nv12`; `hevc-profile=main10`, `Profile`, `MaxVideoBitDepth` and hardware HEVC decoding changed nothing). So a capped HDR title is SDR or 8-bit PQ today; the playback info panel says which. One saved preference (`library.streamQuality`), set from the detail page's gauge button before Play or from the player's Quality menu; `JellyfinLibrary.streamPlan(itemId:quality:)` builds the stream, and `playbackRoute(for:)` uses the preference, so tvOS's system player honours fixed caps too (the Mac plays Library titles in the video window). The level now playing is read back off the URL's `MaxStreamingBitrate` (`level(ofStream:)`), so it survives renewed play sessions and restored windows. Auto runs only in the video window (`VideoWindowModel+StreamQuality`): both AVPlayer paths report `waiting` (`.waitingToPlayAtSpecifiedRate`, `RAVEPlaybackState.waiting`), and `StreamStallMonitor` steps down after no first frame in 12 s, one 6 s stall, or 10 s of stalls within 90 s, ignoring the 4 s after a seek, resume or stream switch and the 15 s after a step. The step is announced by `StreamQualityNotice`, hung under the window beside the skip button (the ornament `prompt` slot). The object-audio film player has its own picture tiers (below). With **Play MKV Files on This Device** on (`JellyfinLibrary.remuxesOnDevice`, Jellyfin settings, on by default) an MKV the native player can't open plays as its own file, remuxed on the device as a Stash MKV is ("Matroska remux" in `hypnos-video-window.md`), rather than as Jellyfin's HLS remux: every audio track, the original sound, no server work. The server's remux is the plan's reserve `transcodeURL`, for a file the device declines.

**The player's Quality menu lists what the title can be streamed at** (2026-10-23), not the whole ladder: `LibraryStreamQuality.offered` (RAVEMediaCore) drops levels above the source's picture class (`pictureClass`: by either side, so a 1920×800 film is 1080p) and levels whose cap is above the source's bitrate (they'd play the original). The window asks `JellyfinLibrary.sourcePicture` (PlaybackInfo's video stream width/height/frame rate and the source bitrate) once per title (`loadLibrarySource`). A saved level the title doesn't offer checks Original (that's what plays); the badge shows what plays ("1080p" for an original 1080p film, never the preference's "4K"). The detail page's gauge still sets the cross-title preference from the full ladder.

## Jellyfin 12.x

Jellyfin 12 dropped the legacy auth spellings: the `X-Emby-Token`/`X-Emby-Authorization` headers and the `api_key` query answer 401/400. Send `Authorization: MediaBrowser … Token="…"` and `ApiKey=`, which 10.11 also accepts. The plugin targets 12.2 / .NET 10 and doesn't load on 10.11, so a server runs the plugin build for its version. The dev script runs 12.2 (Docker and native; native with jellyfin-ffmpeg's portable build, which has the Dolby Vision filters Homebrew's lacks).

## Subtitles

Every player draws subtitles itself with RAVEUI's `RAVESubtitleOverlay`; none of them has an `AVPlayerItem` whose legible output could. `SubtitleController` (`Services/Subtitles/`, one per `FilmSession` and per `VideoWindowModel`) lists an item's tracks, fetches and parses the chosen one and holds the timing offset; `SubtitleSource` is where tracks come from: Jellyfin's `Videos/{id}/{source}/Subtitles/{n}/Stream.vtt` (converts sidecar SRT/ASS and embedded text streams). Embedded PGS comes as `Stream.pgssub`, a `.sup` file decoded by `PGSSupFile` with Vergence's PGS decoder (10.11 can't convert images to text); the first request for an embedded track extracts every subtitle stream from the whole file, minutes on a UHD remux, and 15–90 s for an Archer episode on a network share. So an embedded **text** track (`LibrarySubtitleTrack.isEmbedded`) is **streamed from the plugin** instead (`JellyfinLibrary.subtitleStream`, the plugin's `FilmSubtitles/{id}/{index}.vtt?startSeconds=`; `JellyfinPlugin/README.md` → Subtitles): ffmpeg seeks to the playhead and the cues arrive as the file reads, `SubtitleController` shows them as they come (`SubtitleSource.progressive`, the owner's `playhead`), restarts on a seek outside what has arrived, and fills in the stretch before the run once it reaches the end. A server without the plugin answers 404 and the track is fetched whole as before; sidecars always are. **An embedded PGS track streams too** (since 2026-11-04; Parasite's English PGS took about four minutes the whole-file way): the plugin's `FilmSubtitles/{id}/{index}.sup` copies the stream from the playhead, `JellyfinLibrary.subtitleStream` cuts what has arrived at whole display sets (`PGSSupFile.DisplaySetScanner`), and `PGSSupFile.track(isGrowing:)` holds back the last caption until the set that ends it has come, or the merge would keep it twice. A plugin from before the route answers 404, so it falls back to `Stream.pgssub`. The harness covers it on the seed's `Caption Window (2026)` (synthetic PGS from `scripts/gen-pgs-sup.py`, as ffmpeg has no PGS encoder). The `-LibraryHarness` check `streamedSubtitles` covers resumed / from-start / kept runs on the dev seed's `Subtitle Window (2026)` (ten minutes of MKV with an embedded SRT). Other image formats (VobSub, DVB) are listed but disabled or Stash's sidecar captions (`scene.captions` + `paths.caption?lang=&type=`, served as WebVTT). An MKV/WebM that plays remuxed on the device adds its own tracks (`SubtitleSource.matroska`, read near the playhead from the file itself; see "Matroska remux" in `hypnos-video-window.md`). A video file on the device (Local library, shared files) gets `SubtitleSource.localFile` (`LocalSubtitleSource.swift`): SRT/VTT files beside it whose name starts with the video's (`Film.fr.forced.srt`; the words in between give language and flags), plus `tx3g` text tracks inside an MP4/MOV, read with AVAssetReader and handed to the parser as SubRip (other embedded formats list as unsupported). `SubtitlePreference` carries the last chosen language between films (normalised to the two-letter code, since Jellyfin says `eng` and Stash `en`), and which of that language's tracks it was: its title, then SDH and forced (2026-11-05: a Netflix episode's "English, For English audio" fell back each episode to the default "For Japanese audio"); until the user has chosen, only a forced track shows. `SubtitleMenu` sits in the film transport and the video ornament (the Audio menu beside it picks the title's audio stream: `AudioStreamIndex` on the remux/transcode URL, see "Audio menu" in `hypnos-video-window.md`); **the menu always has Choose Subtitle File…** (not tvOS, which has no document picker; `SubtitleController.offersMenu`), so a video with no subtitle tracks and no AI captions still gets one: an SRT or WebVTT file (UTF-8, Windows-1252 or UTF-16) is parsed by `SubtitleController.loadFile` into a track named after the file and shown at once. It is never remembered as the language preference, stays through a reload of the same video's source (`load(_:keepingPickedFile: true)`, the remux opening) and is dropped for the next video. The timing offset (`SubtitleDelayControl`: slider, ±0.1 s buttons, Reset) is in that menu while a track shows, and nowhere else but tvOS's own Sound panel and Subtitles menu; it used to be in the film's Sound and Picture window, the video Adjustments window and More's rows. The picker is reported to the chrome as an open menu so auto-hide can't remove the view the importer hangs on. The tvOS generic player (`TVVideoPlayerView`, `AVPlayerViewController`, which plays every non-film Library title, Archer included) draws them in the controller's `contentOverlayView` (inside the picture, under the transport bar) and offers a Subtitles menu with the timing offset in `transportBarCustomMenuItems`; it had no subtitles at all before 2026-10-20. It also deselects AVKit's own legible option while one of ours shows, so a direct-play MP4's text track isn't drawn twice.

**Appearance** (2026-11-06): one saved look for every player, `subtitles.style` (a RAVEUI `RAVESubtitleStyle` as JSON; empty = the default, white semibold on a 60% black plate): size, weight, font design, text colour and opacity, plate colour and opacity, edge (none/shadow/outline) and distance from the picture's edge. RAVEUI's `RAVESubtitleText`/`Overlay`/`Panel` read it from `\.raveSubtitleStyle`; `.subtitleStyled()` (`SubtitleAppearanceEditor.swift`) injects the saved one on `SubtitleOverlay` and `SubtitleBand`, and `StereoSubtitleFeeder` passes it into its `ImageRenderer` (an environment doesn't reach it) and republishes when it changes. Edited in Settings → Subtitles (all platforms; tvOS uses checkmark rows, no Picker/Slider/ColorPicker) and in a sheet from the subtitle menu's "Subtitle Appearance…" (not tvOS). The HDR brightness slider for 3D subtitles (`subtitles.hdrWhiteNits`) is on the same page on visionOS. A speaker's colour (AI Captions: speaker 1 default, others a palette, `SubtitleController.speakerText`) is a run attribute and wins over the text colour. Bitmap (PGS) subtitles aren't restyled. Not yet checked on a device.

Placement: inside the aspect-fitted picture on flat players; **drawn into both eyes of the picture** while it is stereo (fake-3D or live 3D, video window and film player), because SwiftUI drawn over the stereo video plane never shows in front of it, whatever its z offset (device, 2026-10-07: the overlay at +20 pt was invisible), and the band hung under the window that replaced it sat at the window's depth, so the picture covered it from an angle. `StereoSubtitleFeeder` renders the cues showing with `RAVESubtitleText` into images at eye-pixel size and hands them to the engine; RAVEMedia's `StereoSubtitles.swift` places them in front of the content behind them (see RAVESDK `CLAUDE.md`). Their HDR white is `@AppStorage("subtitles.hdrWhiteNits")` (default 203 nits) and a user depth offset `subtitles.stereoDepth` (−1 nearer … 1 farther, ±0.004 of the eye's width per eye) moves them off the automatic depth. `subtitles.stereoStyle` = `belowPicture` brings back the band hung under the window (`SubtitleBand` in `PlayerOrnamentStack`, `subtitles.bandGap` below the edge, adjustable, default 20 pt). All three live in `StereoSubtitleSettings`, in the video Adjustments window and the film's Sound and Picture window while 3D is on.

Jellyfin 3D movies (`Video3DFormat`, e.g. `Movie.3D.HSBS.mkv`; mapped by `StereoscopicFormat(jellyfin:)` onto `LibraryItem.stereoFormat`) play as **live 3D**: the generic route hands the format to the video window (`LibraryPlaybackCoordinator.makeGalleryVideo` sets `isStereoscopic`/`stereoscopicFormat`), and an object-audio film's `FilmSession.stereoFormat` makes `FilmPlayerView` open in 3D with the eyes cropped from its sample-buffer tap (no depth model). A **Blu-ray disc** (Jellyfin `VideoType` Iso + `IsoType` BluRay, or a BDMV folder; `LibraryItem.isBlurayDisc`) with object audio opens in the film player with its picture decoded on the device: `MVCPictureSource` reads the raw ISO from `Videos/{id}/stream?static=true` with HTTP ranges through the local `vergence` package (`../../vergence`, product `VergenceApple`; MVC 3D via edge264) and feeds RAVEFilm's `FilmPictureSource` path; the plugin still supplies the audio, reading the disc through `vergence stream` (see `JellyfinPlugin/README.md`). Film time = playlist time + the first PlayItem's IN time, which is also the audio scene's `startSeconds`. Every Jellyfin disc goes to the film player (`playbackRoute`). When the plugin has no object audio for it (no plugin, or no TrueHD), the sound is the disc's own AC-3 (`DiscAudioSource`): a plain AC-3/E-AC-3 track, or the AC-3 core inside TrueHD, decoded through Apple's decoder by Vergence's `PlaylistAudioReader`. It plays as a RAVEFilm `FilmAudioSource` scene: five objects pinned at the 5.1 speaker positions plus an LFE bed, in 4 s segments read apart from the picture. The `filmDiscAudio` debug command switches a playing disc to it. A **disc image on the device** (`.iso` in the Local library) opens the same way, with no server: each platform's video-window root shows `LocalDiscHandoffView`, which calls `FilmSession.loadLocalDisc`, opens the film player and closes itself. Local film ids start with `local:` (`FilmSession.isServerItem`), so nothing is reported to Jellyfin. The disc's **PGS subtitles** come from the same reads as the picture (`DiscSubtitles`, fed by `MVCPictureSource`'s side PIDs) and are offered by `SubtitleSource.disc`. Cues carry a `RAVESubtitleImage`, decoded only while it shows, and the flat overlay and the stereo picture (`StereoSubtitle.frame`) place it where the disc put it. **A Jellyfin HLS remux reopens in a new play session after the background** (`VideoWindowModel.renewJellyfinStreamAfterBackground`, `JellyfinLibrary.renewedStreamURL`): a remux resumed mid-file cuts its segments at 6 s steps from the resume point while Jellyfin's playlist steps from the start of the file, so later segments sit 0–5 s from where the playlist puts them. Played straight through that is invisible; a segment reload (visionOS takes the decoder away during the low-light alert) shifted the player's clock, and subtitles with it, 3–5 s behind speech. A new job starts exactly at the first segment asked for; the stereo player opens it at the playhead (`takeStreamSwitchStart`) rather than playing from 0 and seeking, which stalled the picture until the next keyframe. The dev Jellyfin seeds `Twin Horizon (2020).3D.HSBS.mp4` (embedded mov_text + sidecar French SRT), and the macOS `-LibraryHarness` prints each 3D movie's format; sidecar English/Spanish SRTs sit on the Atmos demo.

## AI captions

AI captions for **on-device MKV/WebM remuxes** read the selected audio
rendition's fMP4 fragments directly (`MatroskaCaptionAudio`), starting near the
playhead and following audio selections and seeks. `GeneratedCaptions` keeps a
single codec decoder across fragments and restores absolute PTS using
Vergence's `FragmentTimes`: separate AVAssetReader PCM decoders reset time and
trim AAC priming at every boundary. Audio fragments alone are read, never the
whole file. This bypasses `AVPlayerItemSampleBufferOutput`, which delivered no
audio on visionOS 27.2 despite the same code working on macOS.

**Volatile and final results** (`CaptionWorker.receive`, fixed 2026-10-29): a volatile result is the recognizer's whole current guess from the last final on, as ONE run spanning the stretch (`6.92-10.26`); the final that settles it arrives word by word over a narrower range (`6.98-8.96`). Removing only the words inside the final's range left the guess behind, so a line showed twice ("I'll be your proxy… I'll be a proxy…"), with speaker detection on or off. A volatile result now replaces the previous volatile words (`volatileWords`), and a final drops the guesses it covers. `overlappingResultsDoNotRepeatWords` (`CAPTION_INTEGRATION=1 CAPTION_FIXTURE=<a speech file over ~15 s>`, e.g. from `say -o`) fails on the old merge and passes now.

**Caption lines** (`CaptionWorker.lines(from:)`, 2026-11-07). Finals come a word to a run with a leading space; **a volatile guess is ONE run over its whole range, whose first word has no leading space**, and whose range runs on to wherever audio has been fed (3.84-12.11 s for text spoken by 8 s). That gave "Sentence ends here.and another begins" (the join added nothing), a whole guess on screen at once, and lines that reflowed when the finals replaced it. Now: `join` adds the missing space (not in Han/kana/Thai, not before closing punctuation); `timedWords` spreads a volatile guess's words at speech pace from its start (0.05 s + 0.055 s a character, squeezed only to fit the range; one-word runs and unspaced text stay whole); a final that reaches past the guess's start drops it whole (`volatileStart`), as the single run used to be; a result's leading punctuation-only run ("..") is dropped. Lines break where the speech does, not greedily from the head of the list (which moved every later line when the window's 60 s edge pruned a word): a pause of 0.7 s, another speaker, or a sentence end once the unit has 24 characters (so "Okay." keeps the next sentence). A unit over 90 characters or 4 s is cut into even parts at its sentence ends and commas, never leaving a word or two alone. `CaptionLineTests` cover joins, glued sentences, breaks, anchoring under pruning and the pacing. Checked against `say` speech on the Mac; **not yet on the headset**. Recognition itself varies between runs of the same audio (it dropped "and another one begins right after" in one), which no line logic can fix.

**Volatile guesses showed before the line was said** (fixed 2026-11-07). A guess's range opens at the end of the last final, i.e. in the pause before the speech, and audio is analysed ~8 s ahead, so its words (placed from the range start) went on screen during the silence and again at the right time when the final came. `VoiceActivity` (`DialogueAudio.swift`) tracks speech in the fed audio (20 ms frames 10 dB over a noise floor that rises 3 dB/s, above −50 dBFS; gaps under 0.15 s bridged, stretches under 0.12 s ignored), and `receive` starts a guess's words at the first speech in its range; with none found it keeps the range start. Finals keep the recognizer's own times.

**Dialogue channel** (`DialogueMixer`, 2026-11-07). The recognizer and diarizer hear one channel. `AVAudioConverter` to mono keeps only the centre of 5.1 (measured: L/R/LFE/surrounds dropped entirely) and the left of stereo. The mixer keeps the centre alone while it carries sound (music and effects stay out), but when the centre's decaying energy (~30 s memory) is 20 dB under the front pair (stereo carried as 5.1, some TV mixes) it adds the front pair; stereo is averaged; LFE and surrounds never go in. Labels come from the format's channel layout (tag, bitmap or descriptions), else WAVE order. The switch is logged ("centre channel empty, listening to the front pair"). Captions read whichever rendition the player plays, so with Stable Audio on they hear the levelled "(Stable)" copy. `DialogueAudioTests` cover both; **neither checked on the headset yet**.

**DTS and TrueHD remuxes (ALAC) failed every time until 2026-11-07.** A sideload MKV whose audio is DTS or TrueHD plays remuxed as 24-bit ALAC renditions, and `FragmentedCaptionAudio`'s packet converter (`AVAudioConverter` over compressed buffers, built for AAC's priming) rejected each ALAC packet as bad data: log `AI captions: failed (code 560226676); audio ahead 0.0` (560226676 is `'!dat'`, `kAudioCodecBadDataError`), 0.2 s to 8 s after "independent audio decoder". Reproduced on the Mac with the remux's own `alac.mkv` fixture; neither unwrapping the `frma`/`alac` cookie nor zeroing `mVariableFramesInPacket` changed it. ALAC now goes through the reader's own PCM output (`decodesInReader`), which has no priming to trim; AAC, AC-3 and the rest keep the one-converter path. `FragmentedAudioCodecTests` (a 32 KB ALAC fragment in `Tests/.../Fixtures/alac-fragment`) covers it; the fixture is stereo 16-bit, so a 5.1 ALAC track (a real DTS film) is **not yet checked on the headset**.

**Every other HLS stream is read the same way** (2026-10-29, `StreamCaptionAudio`
→ `HLSCaptionAudio` in the package): YouTube and a Jellyfin remux or transcode
had fallen back to the OS output and failed on the headset with "This stream
isn't providing decoded audio to AI captions" (log: "OS HLS audio output", then
"hold timed out; audio ahead 0.0"). The playlists are read with the stream's
credentials (`MediaAuthorization`), the rendition the player has selected is
matched by NAME then LANGUAGE (`HLSPlaylist.rendition`), and only segments from
the playhead on are fetched. Two containers: **fragmented MP4** (an `EXT-X-MAP`;
Jellyfin's `SegmentContainer=mp4`, muxed with the picture, so its video bytes
are fetched and ignored) through `fragmentedMP4`, and **packed audio** (ADTS AAC
behind an ID3 tag, no init segment; YouTube's audio renditions) through
`packedAudio`, placed by the playlist's own timeline rather than the ID3
timestamp. MPEG-TS segments and encrypted streams fail with their own message.
A progressive file still uses the file reader. The log now says "independent
audio decoder" for all of these. Checked: package tests for the playlist parser
and rendition choice, and `CAPTION_PACKED_FIXTURE=a.aac,b.aac
CAPTION_PACKED_DURATIONS=… swift test --filter PackedCaptionAudioIntegrationTests`
against two real YouTube segments on the Mac. **Not yet checked on the headset
or against a Jellyfin stream.** The Subtitles log reports the route, first decoded audio, and buffer
levels at failure, without URLs or dialogue.

## Library downloads (offline)

`Services/Library/Offline/`. A Download button sits top-right on every Library detail page (`LibraryDownloadButton`), the same choices are in each lockup's context menu (`LibraryDownloadMenuItems`), and the Library home's toolbar opens `LibraryDownloadsView`. tvOS has none of it (`LibraryDownloads.isSupported`: its storage is a purgeable cache).
- **Permission is enforced client-side.** Jellyfin's `CanDownload` (asked for on every list via `Fields`) and the account's `Policy.EnableContentDownloading` grey the button out, and pressing it says why. `Items/{id}/Download` answers 403 for such an account, but the `stream.mp4` remux does not (measured on the dev instance with a `nodownloads` user), so the job checks `canDownload` itself before any request.
- **What gets downloaded** (`JellyfinLibrary.downloadPlan`): an AVFoundation-playable MP4/MOV as the original file; anything else (MKV, WebM) as a progressive server remux to fragmented MP4 (video copied when H.264/HEVC, AAC 5.1; no Content-Length, so progress is against the source size); an object-audio film as a `FilmPackage`, the plugin's own video segments joined into one MP4 plus its whole audio scene (`POST Prepare`, then every segment), played offline by `FilmServerClient(itemID:offline:)` through the streamed code path; a Blu-ray disc as its ISO, plus the package's audio when the plugin has objects.
- **Where:** the media file and its WebVTT subtitle sidecars go to `Documents/Videos/Jellyfin/` (Files app and Local library); metadata, artwork, segments and the package go to `Application Support/LibraryOffline/` (`LibraryOfflineStore`, `index.json`). A file deleted in Files drops its download at the next launch. Opening a film-player download from the Local library hands it to the film player (`GalleryVideo.opensInFilmPlayer`), so its object audio survives.
- **Offline behaviour lives in `JellyfinLibrary`**, so screens and players need no changes: an unreachable server (`isUnreachable`) makes home/item/seasons/episodes/Next Up/next episode answer from `LibraryOfflineCatalog`; a download always plays from the device (`playbackRoute`), with subtitles, segments and artwork from the device. Progress reports also update the record's local position; one the server didn't take sets `needsSync`, and `LibraryDownloads.syncPendingWatchState` sends it (`POST UserItems/{id}/UserData`) at launch and when the server answers again.
- The media transfer is a background `URLSession` (draft records under `pending/` file a transfer that finishes in a later launch); the object-audio package is fetched while the app runs and resumes from `video-ranges.json` on retry.
- `-LibraryDownloadHarness` (macOS DEBUG, `Support/LibraryDownloadHarness.swift`) downloads one title of each kind from the dev Jellyfin, plays each back from the device, serves the Library from an unreachable server, syncs offline progress and checks a downloads-disabled account (`HYPNOS_HARNESS_NODL_TOKEN`/`_USER`).

## Music library and player

`LibraryMusicView` is an inline Library section. Wide windows have Recently Added / Albums / Artists / Songs / Playlists navigation beside a lazy square-artwork grid; compact iPhones use two columns and a section picker. Recently Added groups albums by DateCreated. Album and playlist details load their songs on demand; album membership uses Jellyfin IDs, never the album title. Playlist requests retain server order.

`JellyfinLibrary.musicPage` shares the existing Library actor's authentication, user resolution, HTTP status checks and decoding. Browsing requests are bounded to 60 records, with explicit Load More. Do not restore a whole-catalog songs fetch or count songs by filtering them in an album row. `MusicListing` partitions page results once and owns a coalesced page task independently of a view's lifetime. Listings cache their loaded pages, reset on the Library configuration key, and reject results from a prior account. `MusicCatalog` bounds the number of cached listings. Explicit Play on an album/playlist completes its queue in bounded page requests.

**Music plays in the background because `Info.plist` declares `UIBackgroundModes` `audio`** (2026-11-04). Without it visionOS suspended the app soon after its windows went inactive and the music stopped (Longwave declares the same). The mode keeps the whole app running, so every player that should not play unseen pauses itself on `.background`: the video window's native player (`pauseNativePlayerForBackground`; WebKit and stereo already pause through `isRoomActive`) and the visionOS film player, each unless the Cinema space holds it. Not yet checked on a headset.

`JellyfinMusicSession` owns playback only. The catalog never observes its playback clock. A terminal Next calls `stop`, pausing and detaching the AVPlayer, removing both observers, and then clearing the current item. The mini player reuses RAVEUI's dynamic control strip and shares the tab ornament's vertical stack on visionOS. Speaker/Music mode and head-locked audio remain in the audio options menu.

`scripts/check-music-player.sh` compiles the actual music session and catalog against isolated macOS fixtures. It checks terminal Next cleanup, explicit Stop, songs without album metadata, bounded/cached/coalesced loading, leaving during a load, pagination and account changes. Server contract checks use the disposable Jellyfin on :8097 and its music sample; never use a real server for automated checks.

Shuffle is available in every music section and in album, artist and playlist details. Albums/playlists build their complete scoped queue on explicit Shuffle, permute it once, and advance without repeats. Library/artist/search shuffle requests a bounded server-random Audio page (`playbackSongs` / `randomOrder`) rather than shuffling the currently visible album page; its queue refills at the end. New playback and Stop invalidate any pending refill so it cannot start audio later. The mini player and Now Playing also expose shuffle mode.

The music sidebar uses a fixed icon column, uniform 44-point rows and matching horizontal insets; selection backgrounds stay inside its bounds. The mini player fits inside the window width. Its parent animates insertion/removal (opacity only with Reduce Motion), retaining the current item's value through removal so the artwork/title do not disappear before the transition finishes. Music volume is persisted independently of mute and supplied to RAVEPlayerControls' existing slider popover. Its volume accessory holds the real Sound Check toggle (below).

### Library features (2026-11-04)

Measured against Jellyfin 12.2 on the dev instance; the facts behind every choice below are in the Hypnos memory entry `jellyfin-music-api-findings`.

- **Queue identity is a position.** `JellyfinMusicSession.currentIndex` says where playback is; never look a song up by id (a playlist can hold a song twice, and Jellyfin gives duplicate entries the same `PlaylistItemId`). `playNext`, `addToQueue`, `removeFromQueue`, `moveInQueue` and `jump(to:)` edit the queue and keep the gapless preload (`refreshPreload`) in step; Now Playing's Up Next list uses them (tap, drag, swipe, menu).
- **Sections.** Library: Recently Added, Albums, Artists, Songs, Genres. Collections: Playlists, Favorite Songs, Recently Played, Most Played (server sort/filters, no client lists). Albums and Songs take a sort (`MusicSort`). Songs sort by `Name`: a song's `SortName` starts with its track number. `MusicRequest.genreID` and `.artistID` with `section: .songs` give an item's songs (what Play Next and Add to Playlist use through `MusicActions.songs(of:)`). Search returns artists, albums and songs. The Playlists section asks for `MediaTypes=Audio` so film playlists stay out.
- **Rows and menus.** `MusicSongRow` (track number or artwork, Lossless/Hi-Res capsule from `MediaStreams`, favorite heart, duration, menu); `MusicItemMenu` is the one menu for songs, albums, artists and playlists (Play Next, Add to Up Next, Add to Playlist, Favorite, star rating, Go to Album/Artist, Get Info). Favorites and stars go through `MusicUserState` (optimistic, reverts on failure); stars are `UserData.Rating` at two points a star, which Jellyfin's web UI ignores.
- **Playlists belong to a user.** Create, add, rename, reorder (`Move/{n}`), remove and delete all need a signed-in session; on a bare API key Jellyfin answers 400, so `JellyfinLibrary.hasUserSession` gates the UI and the calls throw `MusicServerError.signInRequired` first. Arranging uses Edit mode with move up/down and remove buttons.
- **Play counts.** Jellyfin counts every stop of a song as a play, a skip included (`Failed: true` doesn't help). `JellyfinMusicSession.finishReporting` reads the song's play state before a stop that is short of a real listen (`isCountedListen`: half the song or four minutes) and writes it back after, so Most Played and Recently Played reflect listening.
- **Sound Check** (`jellyfinMusic.soundCheck`) multiplies the volume by `10^(NormalizationGain/20)`, clamped to at most 1 (AVPlayer can't boost), per song at each gapless boundary. It only has something to apply when the Jellyfin music library has its loudness scan on **and** the "Audio Normalization" scheduled task has run; the mini player's volume popover says when the playing song has no data. `AlbumNormalizationGain` stayed null on the dev instance.
- **Lyrics** (`MusicLyricsView`, a pane in Now Playing): `Audio/{id}/Lyrics` gives lines with `Start` ticks and, from enhanced LRC, word `Cues`; the active line follows the playhead and the words light as they are sung.
- **Get Info** (`MusicInfoEditor`, administrators only: `Policy.IsAdministrator`) edits the item as the server holds it and changes only the touched fields. The edit lives in Jellyfin's database, never in the audio file, and a refresh or library scan undoes it unless the item is locked, so "Keep my edits" sets `LockData` (it protects every field; per-field `LockedFields` only covers Name, Genres, Tags, Overview and a few more). "Re-read From File" unlocks, clears the fields a file may never have had (comment, sort title, tags) and refreshes with `replaceAllMetadata`. Not built: multi-song editing (no bulk endpoint, so N saves), cover upload, Identify against MusicBrainz (needs the server's internet providers).

**iTunes-style browsing (2026-11-05).**
- **Sort and direction.** `MusicRequest.sort` plus `descending` (nil = the order's own direction: newest, most played and latest year first). Orders: Title, Artist, Album, Year, Time, Plays, Last Played, Date Added, per section in `MusicSection.sorts`; `defaultSort` is what a section is in until picked. `LibraryMusicView` remembers each section's choice (`MusicSortPreference`, UserDefaults). Only top-level lists sort (`isTopLevel`); the inside of an album/artist/playlist/genre keeps its own order.
- **Section headers follow the order** (`MusicGrouping.of`): letters (articles The/A/An skipped, as Jellyfin's `SortRemoveWords` does), artist, album, year, or Today/Yesterday/This Week/This Month/"MMMM yyyy" for date added (Recently Added too). Headers are *runs* of the server's order, never a dictionary grouping, so a header is not moved up to meet an earlier run. Time, Plays, Last Played and search have none.
- **Songs table.** At 760 pt and wider the top-level song lists (Songs, Favorite Songs, Recently Played, Most Played) become columns Name / Artist / Album / Plays / Time under a pinned `MusicSongColumnHeader`; clicking a column sorts by it and flips the direction on the sorted one. `MusicColumns` holds the widths header and rows share.
- **Fuzzy search.** The matcher is RAVESDK's `RAVESearch` (`RAVEFuzzyIndex`; Worldcast's matcher generalised, design notes in RAVESDK's CLAUDE.md "RAVESearch"). `MusicSearchIndex` only configures it: weights name 3 / artist 1.5 / album 1, `primaryExactBonus` 6, `primaryPrefixBonus` 3, `joinPrimaryWords` ("AC/DC" is also "acdc"), priority artist < album < song then shorter name. `MusicSearchStore` builds it from `musicSearchPage` (names only, 500 per page, never artwork/user data) the first time someone types in the search field, saves the docs under Caches/MusicSearch keyed by the configuration (hashed), and on later launches loads them and asks only for the three totals; rebuilt when they change, after 3 days, or after Get Info edits names (`libraryChanged(namesChanged:)`). Until it is ready search falls back to the server's `SearchTerm` and the screen asks again when `generation` changes. Hits are resolved to full items with `musicItems(ids:)` and keep the index's ranking. Don't re-implement matching here; change the options or RAVESearch. `scripts/check-music-player.sh` compiles `../RAVESDK/Sources/RAVESearch` with the session and catalog.
- **Mini Player** is RAVEUI's drawing (`RAVEMiniPlayerPanel` for the window, `RAVEMiniPlayerBar` for the compact bar over the library; Longwave's `AudioPlayerPanel` shape, generalised) around the shared `MusicTransportStrip`; `MusicMiniPlayerView` and `MusicMiniPlayerWindowView` only supply the music session's artwork, labels and controls. The **window** (macOS and visionOS, scene id `music-mini-player`) adds favorite, stars, an Up Next popover and the item menu. Opened from Now Playing's toolbar, the mini player's option row, or the Mac Music menu (Shift-Command-M). Now Playing and the window host their own `MusicActions` through `musicActionHost`, so Add to Playlist, Get Info and the notice work there too.
- **Repeat** cycles Off / All / One (`MusicRepeatMode`, persisted); Repeat One keeps no gapless preload and restarts the song when it ends, while a manual Next still moves on. **Clear Up Next** keeps the queue through the playing song and stops a random refill. **Save as Playlist** hands the queue to the playlist picker. **Start Station** (item menu) plays Jellyfin's `Items/{id}/InstantMix`.
- **Mac Music menu** shortcuts all carry Option so they leave text-field editing keys alone; plain Space is deliberately not a menu shortcut (it would swallow typed spaces).
